Weak formulation and Birkhoff weighting vs Timeseries Foundation models

I took Lorenz63 as a benchamark to explore how different existing methods do at forcasting chaotic dynamics... I asked: Does the weak formualtion give us the robustness we need? Does weighted Birkhoff averaging from front page SIAM news help even more? And does any method do any better than SINDy with its tuned prior...?

Empirically we saw that, weak-form SINDy gives up some clean-data efficiency but degrades much more slowly as training noise rises. Giving less weight to windows near the ends of a trajectory does not add a consistent benefit...

Weak equations

SINDy fits a vector field from observed states. Estimating their time derivatives amplifies noise. A weak formulation fits integrated equations instead:

\[-\int x(t)\phi'(t)\,\mathrm{d}t =\int\Theta(x(t))\Xi\phi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.\]

The test function \(\phi\) vanishes at the window boundaries. Its derivative replaces the noisy data derivative. The library \(\Theta\) contains candidate terms, and \(\Xi\) holds their fitted coefficients.

Endpoint weighting gives less influence to windows near the ends of a trajectory. For a window centered at relative position \(s\), the taper is

\[w(s)=\exp\!\left(4-\frac{1}{s(1-s)}\right),\qquad 0<s<1.\]

It weights the regression rows while leaving each local weak integral unchanged.

Reading the curves

The benchmark adds Gaussian noise to training observations. It tests forecasts from held-out initial conditions.

A forecast remains valid until its normalized squared error exceeds 0.4. The horizontal axis measures time in Lyapunov units.

At 5% noise, weak SINDy has a restricted mean valid prediction time of 4.76 Lyapunov units over the evaluation horizon. Strong SINDy reaches 3.04.

SINDy’s quadratic library already contains the Lorenz terms. The foundation models forecast directly from their input sequence.
